### T4: Already in hand: a 'still needed' item already held, shown by a technical bridge

- **Status:** promising
- **Family:** possession_state
- **Aimed at:** both
- **Clock trap:** no
- **Aliases:** S.inhand, already in hand
- **Pooled misses:** Fable 24/165 (15%), Astra 13/145 (9%)
- **Loopholes:** L-record, L-threshold

A rule orders or chases what is still needed, and a list says an item is outstanding. A first-hand record shows the item is already held, through a technical bridge the solver must make (a controller already running a known board; a vendor's job sheet for the scanned specimen). The default orders or chases the item. It dies when possession is stated. Measured here (build_evidence.py, 2026-10-15): final 10x2 pooled Fable 13/70, Astra 8/70; all stages Fable 24/165, Astra 13/145 (AMBIGUOUS/DEFECT units excluded). Audited GENUINE in 2 task(s). Status promising: short of proven: no model with pooled final rate >= 0.2 at n >= 20; not dead (some model above 0.05 or n < 20).

**Recipe**

- *record:* a still-needed list or chase line that names the item as outstanding
- *anchors:* two first-hand records whose technical content shows the item is held (a log line, a job sheet, an adviser's identification of the object)
- *withheld:* no sentence says the item arrived or is owned
- *dials:*
  - one semantic bridge per notch; two bridges were disclosed as the soft point in cnc 2.6.0
- *conservation:* grade the order or chase list as an exact set
- *shields:* scope any 'chase everything on the list' rule to items not yet received

**Proof obligations (authoring/validate.py)**

- uniqueness: exactly one listed item is shown held
- ablation: each bridge anchor is necessary (executed)
- no-statement grep: no line states possession

**Hazards**

- two semantic bridges were disclosed as the soft point: cnc 2.6.0 P5
- measured on two tasks only: cnc P5 (Fable) and psychopathy 2.4.0 P6 (Astra, templated T4 by its author)

**What killed it**

- possession stated outright: kibo 2.5.0/2.6.0 P5 commander scope, Fable 0/10
- possession inferred from one incidental remark: kibo 2.7.0 P5 0/5; blockprint P2 stock in the lock-up 0/10
- brightwater 1.4.0-1.6.0 P5 (T4 build): Fable 0/20 finals

**Trace signals**

- the user says 'I already have that' after a list is drafted
- shopping or procurement lists built from a template
- equipment or documents arriving by a side channel

**Domains**

- maker and workshop builds
- research data management
- web content launches
- budget card builds

**Example.** A build list still orders a controller kit, though the printer log shows the owner's own board already running the matching firmware on all four axes.

### T1: Keying slip: a record filed under an ID it does not belong to

- **Status:** proven
- **Family:** record_reversal
- **Aimed at:** both
- **Clock trap:** no
- **Aliases:** ID.slip, keying slip, governing_vs_filing_key
- **Pooled misses:** Fable 742/1835 (40%), Astra 602/1490 (40%)
- **Loopholes:** L-record, L-join, L-union, L-threshold

A structured record (export row, log entry, filed note) carries an ID that is not its owner's. The default joins on the printed key and prices, counts or routes the record under that ID. The files make that impossible: two or more first-hand remarks from different parties describe the record's content, and only the true owner fits. Fable usually notices the conflict and keeps the record anyway; Astra joins silently. Measured here (build_evidence.py, 2026-10-15): final 10x2 pooled Fable 345/680, Astra 321/670; all stages Fable 742/1835, Astra 602/1490 (AMBIGUOUS/DEFECT units excluded); without unaudited >=90% units, final Fable 297/610, Astra 257/580. Audited GENUINE in 13 task(s). Status proven: audited GENUINE in 13 tasks; pooled final miss rate >= 0.2 with n >= 20 on fable, astra (unaudited >= 90% units excluded).

**Recipe**

- *record:* an official-looking keyed record (export row, dispatch line, inbox row) whose own content fits its printed key well enough to pass a casual check
- *anchors:* two or more by-the-way remarks from different parties, each in a document read for another purpose, that describe the record's content and fit only the true owner; at least one makes the printed owner impossible (a date, a location, a transfer)
- *withheld:* no sentence gives the key or record priority, and no sentence names the mechanism of the slip (no 'typo', no 'page-number slip'); the solver supplies the mechanism
- *dials:*
  - one by-the-way anchor per notch toward solvable (a witness of the event or a unique content match)
  - for Astra change the KIND of evidence, not the amount
  - never add a descriptor only the true owner has: it flips the bite to 0
- *conservation:* when the record could be credited to both IDs, grade both sides against a conserved total so the union answer loses
- *shields:* if a tempting rule exists, scope it away from the keyed variable explicitly

**Proof obligations (authoring/validate.py)**

- uniqueness: solve.py enumerates the candidate owners and exactly one fits every anchor
- ablation: dropping each anchor leaves the owner underdetermined or the default (executed)
- no-priority grep: no fixture sentence binds the printed key (audits withdrew psychopathy 2.1.0, ktp 2.1.0, cafe 2.1.0 for one)
- additivity: the keyed reading fails exactly this premise's checks (design_lint)
- blind reader before any dial when a model misses 5/5

**Hazards**

- a sentence that blesses the record makes it AMBIGUOUS: psychopathy 2.1.0 P4 ('the log decides'), ktp 2.1.0 P2 ('the class journal records each lesson'), cafe 2.1.0 P10 (clause 2.2 'drawings govern')
- two records that conflict symmetrically with nothing to decide between them: coloring 2.2.0 P2/P5/P8 AMBIGUOUS, fixed by one first-hand remark in 2.3.0
- a column whose provenance is unstated while a rule defers to it: probation 2.2.0 P4 (AMBIGUOUS, re-audit UNCLEAR)
- a later dated note that supports the printed label: feast 1.1.0 P3 AMBIGUOUS
- a graded label that exists in no file: line-colour 1.0.0 P2 DEFECT
- a 'credit both' reading left open when nothing makes the printed owner impossible: ats 1.1.0 and 1.6.0 P2, closed by an impossibility anchor in 1.7.0
- often flat: 10 of the 19 audited units in the 45-task pass were missed at least 90% by Fable (kibo P3, pathway P2, ats P2, suitcase P5)

**What killed it**

- one descriptor only the true owner has: cnc 2.4.0/2.5.0 P3 'still coiled, wire-tied' took 15/15 to 0/5 on both models
- a specific first-hand noun: coloring 2.4.0/2.5.0 'little crow' took Fable to 2/15, 4/15, 0/15
- a new note placed beside the anchor primes a destination check: gst 1.5.0 P1 11/15 to 0/5 with byte-identical premise text
- one origin remark: gst 1.5.0 P4 'lorry up from Hubballi' 13/15 to 0/5
- kibo 2.8.0 P3 dial (hand-typed name route) overshot to 0/5 on both

**Trace signals**

- the user corrects which file, row, ID or person an item belongs to
- two lists keyed by IDs that are easy to transpose (adjacent register lines, consecutive slide or order numbers)
- a record built by someone other than the people who saw the event (data entry from paper slips, an export of hand-typed names)

**Domains**

- school registers and option slips
- procurement and goods received
- applicant tracking
- production logs and art boards
- small-business bookkeeping

**Example.** An export row keyed to a learner who transferred out three days before slips were collected carries a combination that the homeroom teacher's note and a club leader's remark both tie to a classmate whose slip is missing from the export.

### T6: Which edition or rate applies, by the real date (sources of similar authority)

- **Status:** promising
- **Family:** authority_selection
- **Aimed at:** both
- **Clock trap:** no
- **Aliases:** T.edition, version_in_force
- **Pooled misses:** Fable 96/245 (39%), Astra 113/140 (81%)
- **Loopholes:** L-record, L-shield

Two editions, briefs or rates could govern; the log's key or date points to one, and first-hand dated records show the request really arrived under the other. The default trusts the log's date or the newest edition. It bites both models when the competing sources have similar authority; it is unfair when an informal remark contradicts an official printed form. **Recipe**

- *record:* a log or register that keys the job to an edition by its entry date
- *anchors:* first-hand dated records (a fitting date, a recording made on a named day) that place the real receipt in the other edition's window
- *withheld:* no mapping rule from date to edition is stated
- *dials:*

- *conservation:* the wrong edition must flip at least one graded verdict
- *shields:* never let a printed current form state the opposite of the answer

**Proof obligations (authoring/validate.py)**

- authority parity: the competing sources are of similar standing (audit)
- uniqueness: the dated records fit only one edition window
- blind reader: if the reader misses it too, treat as unfair

**Hazards**

- an informal remark against an official printed form is unfair: home-bp 2.6.0/2.7.0 P5, blind reader also missed (UNCONFIRMED)
- the first blind reader missed the edition question (cafe 2.1.0: 0.78); a second reached it

**Trace signals**

- the user names which version of a brief, standard or rate card applies
- revisions issued while the job was in progress

**Domains**

- fit-out and kitchen design
- payroll and rate cards
- clinical protocols
- legal and regulatory editions
